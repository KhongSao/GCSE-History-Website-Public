Elizabethan Golden Age (2021)

AQA 8145 · Paper 2B · Elizabethan England

8-mark question · Explain what was important about · AO1/AO2

The question

Explain what was important about Elizabethan England that made it a ‘Golden Age’. [8 marks]

Model answer — Level 4

The idea of a ‘Golden Age’ is important because it reflects the extraordinary cultural flourishing that took place in Elizabethan England, particularly in literature and drama. The period produced some of the greatest works in the English language: William Shakespeare wrote plays such as Hamlet, Romeo and Juliet, and A Midsummer Night’s Dream that explored universal human themes and are still performed worldwide today. Christopher Marlowe, Edmund Spenser, and Philip Sidney contributed poetry and plays of equal distinction. This cultural explosion was made possible by patronage — Elizabeth herself had an acting company (the Queen’s Men), while nobles like Robert Dudley, Earl of Leicester, sponsored writers and artists. The theatre industry grew rapidly: from the first purpose-built public theatre (simply called ‘The Theatre,’ built in 1576) to the Globe in 1599, theatrical entertainment became central to London life and attracted audiences of thousands daily. This cultural achievement was important because it established English as a literary language of European significance and created a legacy that has defined perceptions of the period ever since.

The ‘Golden Age’ label is also important because it reflected genuine achievements in exploration and national prestige that transformed England’s place in the world. Francis Drake became the first Englishman to circumnavigate the globe (1577–1580), returning with vast quantities of Spanish silver and earning a knighthood. Walter Raleigh sponsored expeditions to the Americas, and the first English colonial settlement was attempted at Roanoke in 1585. The defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588 — the greatest naval power in Europe — gave English Protestantism a triumphant narrative and boosted national confidence. These achievements mattered because they helped establish England as an Atlantic and Protestant power rather than a peripheral European nation, setting the foundations for the later British Empire. However, it is worth noting that the ‘Golden Age’ label was partly a retrospective idealisation: for ordinary people, the 1590s were marked by harvest failures, poverty, and high taxation caused by war. The ‘Golden Age’ was more real for the educated elite than for the population at large.
